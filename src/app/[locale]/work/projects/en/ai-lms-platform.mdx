When I moved from the Financial AI Center to the Customer Contact Innovation Department and was getting up to speed on the new team's work,
I found that a great many unit tasks were fragmented and managed by hand.

Our department runs several bot engines for customers and employees
(mainly the customer chatbot, the callbot, and the employee chatbot).
Each channel had its own owner and its own solution,
and each kept training data and management ledgers separately in Excel on local storage.

As a result, the same question could get different answers depending on the channel,
and whenever a new product launched, the owner of every channel had to update their own data, which was an awkward way to work.

## Development Process

I started with a simple single-page Flask dashboard,
but requirements piled up as the business owners began using it.
Things like "I want query statistics by time of day" and "group similar questions together."

So I fine-tuned an embedding model myself with PEFT for sentence similarity analysis and set up a separate inference server,
and I redesigned the frontend from scratch as a **FastAPI + HTMX + JavaScript MPA**, a template-based structure
that makes it light and fast to build multiple pages.

## System Architecture

```text
Operator's browser (HTMX + JS)
    ↓
Internal network Region Proxy
    ↓
AWS EKS (Kubernetes Deployment)
    ↓
FastAPI backend
    ├── MySQL (training data / conversation logs)
    ├── Redis (in-memory cache, temporary task storage)
    └── Embedding model (query similarity analysis)
```

Because of the bank's network, configuring communication between the raw data in other AWS accounts and this project's data was quite tricky.
Coordinating firewall openings, security groups, and load balancer setup with the infrastructure team took longer than I expected.

## Lesson & Learned
I got to listen to what the business teams needed, build the analysis features myself, and go through everything from web server development to deploying on cloud services.

In particular, handling customer data that sometimes exceeded a million records a day, and standardizing the many channel-specific tables with different schemas into one unified standard, was hard but valuable work.
