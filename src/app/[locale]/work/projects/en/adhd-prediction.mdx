## Research Overview

This was a one-year project at the MCL (Mobile Communication Lab) at Yeungnam University.
The earlier ADHD (attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder) is found in childhood, the more effective treatment is,
but the current approach depends solely on clinical assessment by medical professionals, which limits accessibility.

The idea started from a question: if children are asked to focus on a single point for one minute,
could we classify children suspected of ADHD early with vision alone, using subtle movement patterns in the facial video
(**mouth-corner movement, eye movement, head tilting, and so on**)? That could be a real contribution to the medical field.


## Data Collection

I recorded one-minute facial videos of third- and fourth-grade elementary school students.

Collected data:
- Eye movement (eye tracking, blink detection)
- Subtle changes in facial muscle expressions
- Head direction and head movement patterns
- Facial movement from the gyro sensor in a hat the children wore

The ground-truth labels for the ADHD-suspected group were provided by Professor Seung-Pil Jung of the Yeungnam University College of Medicine
(now president of the Korea Institute of Radiological & Medical Sciences).

## Model

I applied an image-processing-based eye-feature extraction technique
(a CNN trained on Korean eye data, combined with Dlib facial landmarks).

To capture temporal patterns I used an LSTM-based time-series classifier,
and I also used facial micro-movement features based on VibraImage Technology as additional features.

## Results

The model reached **70%** classification accuracy for the ADHD-suspected group.

From the analysis, I predicted that performance would improve further with a higher-resolution camera
or more precise sensing equipment. The camera we used at the time had limits in capturing subtle facial movement.

The study found a meaningful correlation between facial movement in one minute of video, while staying still,
and early ADHD symptoms.

## Tech Stack

`Python` `TensorFlow` `LSTM` `CNN` `OpenCV` `MediaPipe` `VibraImage`

## Lesson & Learned

This was a long, year-long study.

I visited three elementary schools myself to collect the video data, and I went through the whole research process:
labeling for training the eye-movement model, model development, and performance analysis.

Because trust in AI and data matters most in the medical field, I came to feel that it demands in-depth research and reliable hypothesis testing.
