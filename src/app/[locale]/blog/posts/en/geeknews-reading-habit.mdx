## The problem

I read GeekNews and HN daily for a while. At some point I realised
I was reading a lot but retaining nothing.

Plenty of scrolling, almost nothing applied.

## My current filter

**1. Will I touch this within the quarter?**

If not, I read the title only. I don't bookmark either —
I've accepted that bookmarking is effectively a declaration that I won't read it.

**2. Are there benchmark numbers?**

Lots of posts claim "X is faster than Y." Few state their conditions.
No dataset size, hardware, or version comparison means I skip it.

**3. Is it a failure story?**

Failure stories beat success stories by a wide margin.
"Here's how we succeeded" is hard to reproduce.
"Here's how we broke things" is immediately avoidable.

## Something that actually paid off

Last year I read about a company blowing up their bill through a misconfigured
Kubernetes HPA. It felt like someone else's problem at the time.
Six months later I hit a similar situation and recalled it instantly.

Even for things you won't use now, remembering "there's a trap here somewhere"
gives you a search keyword later. That's enough.

## How I do it now

Thirty minutes on the weekend, batched. Reading daily turned into
habitually checking for "anything new," which ate the most time.

Afterwards I write a one-line summary in my notes.
If I can't compress it to one line, I didn't understand it.
