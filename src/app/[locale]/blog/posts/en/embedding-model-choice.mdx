## Context

I needed an embedding model for RAG.
Mostly Korean documents, with English technical terms sprinkled in.

## I started with benchmarks

I took a top-ranked model from a leaderboard and wired it up.
Retrieval quality was worse than expected.

The issue was that benchmark data and our data had different characters.
Our documents are long, formal policy text; the benchmarks lean on news and general prose.

## I built my own evaluation set

I pulled 50 real user questions and hand-labelled,
for each one, which chunk *should* be retrieved.

Half a day of work — and the best-spent time in the project.

I measured Recall@5: how often the answer appears in the top five.
For RAG, "is it in the candidate set?" matters more than being ranked first.

## What I found

**1. Multilingual models did better**

Our documents mix in terms like `Kubernetes` and `HPA`.
Korean-only models handled those tokens poorly.

**2. More dimensions wasn't better**

Recall@5 barely differed between a 1024-dim and a 384-dim model,
while storage and query latency differed noticeably.

**3. Don't skip normalisation**

If you use cosine similarity, normalise your embeddings.
Forgetting this made my early results nonsense.

```python
output = model(text, pooling="mean", normalize=True)
```

One argument. Very easy to miss.

## Conclusion

I went with a 384-dim multilingual model.
Lower on the leaderboard, better on our data, and far lighter.

**Fifty examples from your own data beat a leaderboard.**
Half a day is enough to build them.
