## Paper and Research Background

The rapid advancement of Generative AI has brought significant changes to the field of language education. Particularly for languages with unique phonological, morphological, and complex pragmatic contexts like Korean, the role of AI in learning is becoming increasingly important. This paper provides a perspective on the future of Korean education from a multidisciplinary viewpoint encompassing AI-assisted language learning, technology acceptance, and educational ethics. The authors argue that it is necessary to build a responsible educational framework that goes beyond simply using AI as a tool in the Korean education field.

## Core Problem and Authors' Claims

The authors claim that while generative AI can be a powerful auxiliary tool in Korean learning, there are numerous challenges that need to be addressed. The main points are as follows:
1. **Expansion of Learning Areas:** AI can provide personalized learning tailored to learners' goals (such as TOPIK preparation, studying abroad, employment, or cultural interest) across all four areas: listening, speaking, reading, and writing.
2. **Korean Specificity:** The core issue lies in how accurately AI can reflect Korean's unique linguistic and cultural characteristics, such as the honorific system and pragmatic differences based on context.
3. **Educational Responsibility:** AI should function as a "mediated learning resource" that mediates between the learner and the teacher, rather than replacing the teacher.
4. **Ethical Risks:** Major challenges identified include hallucinations, data privacy, validity of assessment, and unequal access due to the digital divide.

## Proposed Method and Key Mechanisms

This paper is not a technical paper proposing specific algorithms, but a conceptual narrative review proposing an educational framework. The analytical framework presented by the authors is centered on the following four dimensions:
* **Korean Language Skills:** How AI processes and conveys the basic structure of language, such as phonology and morphology, to learners.
* **Cultural Features:** Contextual dialogue generation that reflects the pragmatic characteristics of the Korean language.
* **Instructional Design:** Strategies for AI prompt engineering and curriculum integration based on learning objectives.
* **Governance Risks:** Guidelines to control ethical and educational side effects that may occur when using AI.

## Experiments and Main Findings

As this paper is a conceptual narrative review rather than an empirical study conducting quantitative experiments or benchmark evaluations, there are no separate quantified experimental results or measurement data. Instead, the authors analyze through literature synthesis how generative AI can support listening, speaking, reading, and writing areas in scenario forms according to learning objectives such as TOPIK preparation, studying abroad, and employment. The main analytical finding of this study is the theoretical deduction that limitations such as hallucinations and decreased cultural accuracy, which AI may face when handling Korean's phonological, morphological, and pragmatic characteristics, can have negative effects on learners.

## Related Code and Reproduction

This paper was written with the purpose of an academic review, and no official code repository or implementation is provided. Therefore, there is no explicit method for technically reproducing the results of this study or running specific AI models. However, to apply the educational strategies proposed by the authors in practice, prompt design using large language models (LLMs) such as OpenAI's GPT series or Anthropic's Claude would likely need to be performed.

## Technical Implications and Limitations

This study provides practical implications in that it analyzes the impact of generative AI on Korean education from multiple angles.
* **Practical Application:** Korean language educators must recognize that "human-centered educational mediation," where the teacher reviews the results generated by AI rather than accepting them directly, is essential when using AI as a classroom support tool.
* **Limitations:** Since this paper remains a conceptual review, it lacks quantitative data to demonstrate the effectiveness of AI introduction in actual educational settings. Furthermore, as it deals with the general characteristics of generative AI rather than the performance of specific models, there is a limitation in that it cannot immediately reflect technological changes due to model updates.
* **Future Research:** Future research is judged to require performance evaluation of AI models using Korean-specific datasets and validation of the effectiveness of AI-based education targeting actual learners.
