## Paper and Research Background

Online Neural Learning is a setting where models must be updated immediately after receiving each sample as data arrives in a stream. Unlike batch learning, it must continuously adapt to **Concept Drift**, where data distribution changes over time, and avoid **Brittle Gradient Transmission**, where gradient propagation becomes unstable. Additionally, the model must remain well-calibrated at every time step.

The author, Sizhen Niu, acknowledges that the initial version of this research had limitations such as **small static benchmarks**, **shallow models**, **few random seeds**, and a **lack of significance testing**. This revised edition redefines the problem as **true Prequential online learning** and conducts a fully reproducible experiment with 896 runs across 8 streams, 14 techniques, 13 baselines, and 8 random seeds. The benchmarks include 4 real-world datasets from scikit-learn and 4 synthetic streaming tasks: Stationary, Nonlinear, Gradual Drift, and Sudden Drift.

The paper was published on December 31, 2026, at `pub.respai.de` (DOI: 10.17613/nzztb-q4520) and is available via the public PDF (https://pub.respai.de/articles/1-2/is2-3.pdf) and landing page (https://pub.respai.de/articles/1-2/3sniu.html). According to OpenAlex, there are 0 citations, and no authors from domestic institutions have been identified.

## Core Problem and Authors' Claims

**Limitations of Existing Approaches**
- Fixed activation functions (ReLU, LeakyReLU, ELU, Softplus, etc.) fail to adapt to distribution changes in streams.
- Existing online optimizers (Adam, SGD) suffer from unstable gradient paths due to the non-smoothness of activation functions.
- Choosing a single activation function relies on prior knowledge and may not be optimal when data stream characteristics change.

**Authors' Key Claims**
1. **Connecting Activation Smoothness and Online Regret**: Applying Hedge-style routing over a set of smooth activation experts guarantees performance close to the hindsight optimal expert with only a **Logarithmic-Exponential-Weight Overhead**.
2. **Single-Instance Adaptive Updates**: Each expert is updated using Diagonal Adaptive Online Gradients, enabling stable learning without batch statistics.
3. **Adaptive Routing is More Robust than Fixed Activations**: While SOAR achieves the best overall performance in controlled benchmarks, fixed smooth activations (e.g., TeLU) remain competitive on small-scale static data.

These claims are based on the theoretical regret bounds and experimental results described in the abstract. The abstract alone does not provide details on the derivation of formulas, the composition of the expert pool, or the routing weight update rules.

## Proposed Method and Key Mechanisms

**SOAR (Smooth Online Activation Routing) Architecture Overview**
According to the abstract, SOAR consists of the following components:

1. **Smooth Activation Experts Pool**: Multiple smooth activation functions (e.g., variants of Softplus, ELU, TeLU) are treated as independent 'experts'.
2. **Hedge-Style Routing**: Expert weights are updated online, applying exponential weighting based on each expert's loss (e.g., Negative Log-Likelihood) for the current sample.
3. **Single-Instance Adaptive Updates**: Each expert network updates its parameters for every incoming sample using Diagonal Adaptive Gradients (e.g., AdaGrad/RMSProp family).
4. **Mixture Output**: The final output is a weighted average of expert outputs, where routing weights are adjusted inversely to the cumulative loss of each expert.

**Theoretical Guarantees (Based on Abstract)**
- **Prediction-with-Expert-Advice Bound**: Guarantees that the cumulative loss of the mixture model is close to the loss of the hindsight optimal expert, with only a logarithmic term added.
- **Activation Smoothness ↔ Regret Connection**: The degree of smoothness of the activation function affects the variance/curvature of the expert loss, which determines the routing regret bound.

**Differences from Existing Techniques**
| Category | Fixed Activation + Online Optimizer | SOAR |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Activation Selection | Fixed before training | Dynamic selection via online routing |
| Gradient Stability | Dependent on activation non-smoothness | Smooth expert pool + adaptive gradients |
| Theoretical Guarantees | General online convex optimization | Explicit regret bounds under expert advice framework |
| Drift Adaptation | Dependent on optimizer learning rate schedule | Immediate adaptation via automatic expert weight redistribution |

The abstract does not contain specific formulas (routing weight update rules, diagonal adaptive gradient formulas, list of candidate activation functions, etc.), which must be verified in the full paper.

## Experiments and Main Findings

**Experimental Setup (Scope verified from abstract)**
- **Model**: 2-hidden layer online neural network
- **Datasets**: 8 streams (4 real-world + 4 synthetic: Stationary, Nonlinear, Gradual Drift, Sudden Drift)
- **Comparison Techniques**: 14 methods (Fixed activations like ReLU, LeakyReLU, ELU, Softplus, TeLU + Adam/SGD optimizer combinations, including compression ablation)
- **Baselines**: 13 comparison groups
- **Random Seeds**: 8
- **Total Runs**: 8 × 14 × 8 = 896 (fully reproducible)
- **Evaluation Metrics**: Final Accuracy, Negative Log-Likelihood (NLL), Prequential Accuracy
- **Statistical Tests**: Paired Wilcoxon + Holm Correction

**Key Metrics (Cited from abstract)**
- **SOAR Average Final Accuracy**: 92.56% (1st overall)
- **SOAR Average NLL**: 0.199 (1st overall)
- **SOAR Average Prequential Accuracy**: 86.83% (1st overall)

**Statistical Significance**
- **Significant improvement** (p &lt; 0.05, after Holm correction) compared to ReLU, LeakyReLU, ELU, Softplus, Adam, SGD, and compression ablation.
- **Positive improvement** compared to fixed **TeLU activation**, but **Not Statistically Significant**.

**Author's Interpretation (Conclusion from abstract)**
> "Adaptive smooth activation routing is a robust online learning mechanism and is the best overall method in this collection of controlled benchmarks. However, fixed smooth activations remain highly competitive on small-scale static data."

**Reviewer's Interpretation (Based on abstract)**
- The strength of SOAR is presumed to be the **rapid redistribution of expert weights in drift environments**, which mitigates the 'incorrect activation fixation' problem faced by fixed activations.
- The non-significant difference with TeLU suggests that **smooth activation itself is already suitable for online learning**, and the routing overhead may offset benefits on small static data.
- While 896 runs seem sufficient to exclude single-seed volatility, **claims of generalization based on only 4 real-world datasets should be treated with caution**.

## Technical Implications and Limitations

**Technical Significance**
1. **Treating Activation Functions as Learning Targets in Online Learning**: Moving beyond the fixed activation paradigm, defining activation selection as an online decision-making problem and providing theoretical regret bounds is a significant methodological contribution.
2. **Application of Expert Advice Framework to Neural Networks**: Applying the Hedge algorithm to activation routing allows the drift adaptation process to be explained through **interpretable weight changes** (which activation is preferred and when).
3. **Combination with Single-Instance Adaptive Gradients**: Choosing diagonal adaptive gradients that guarantee stable updates without batch normalization statistics is suitable for real-time stream environments.

**Practical Applicability**
- **Pros**: Reduces the burden of activation tuning in streaming classification/regression tasks with frequent concept drift (e.g., fraud detection, recommendation systems, sensor monitoring).
- **Considerations**: Memory/computation increases by the number of experts × number of parameters (number of experts not listed in the abstract). Reducing the expert pool size may be necessary for lightweight edge devices.
- **Hyperparameters**: Additional tuning required for routing learning rates and optimizer hyperparameters per expert.

**Limitations and Future Research Questions**
1. **Benchmark Diversity**: Generalization to large-scale/high-dimensional streams (images, text) is unverified, as only 4 real-world datasets (presumably small sets built into scikit-learn) were used.
2. **Expert Pool Design**: Lack of analysis on which smooth activations to include and the saturation point of performance relative to pool size.
3. **Theory-Experiment Gap**: No analysis on how tight the log-exponential-weight overhead bound is compared to actual NLL/accuracy improvements.
4. **Lack of Recent Baselines**: Comparison with recent SOTA, such as online ensembles (Online Bagging/Boosting), adaptive architectures (Progressive Networks), and meta-learning-based online adaptation, is missing.
5. **Reproducibility Infrastructure**: Uncertainty regarding the release of code, data, and execution scripts may delay community verification.

**Reviewer's Overall Opinion**
SOAR is significant in that it opens 'activation function selection' as a new dimension of adaptation in online learning. However, claims of 'best performance' are limited to controlled environments, given the **small-scale benchmarks, unconfirmed code availability, and lack of comparison with the latest baselines**. Before practical adoption, **pilot experiments on proprietary stream data**, **measurement of the expert pool size-latency trade-off**, and **combination experiments with existing online ensembles** should be prioritized.
