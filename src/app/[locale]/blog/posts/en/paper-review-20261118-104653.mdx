## Why Excessive AI Guidance Is a Problem

As AI becomes increasingly integrated into virtual reality and immersive environments, it can guide user attention more accurately and efficiently. However, this efficiency also entails a design risk that cannot be ignored. Excessive AI guidance can gradually undermine user autonomy and hinder exploratory actions and meaning-making processes. This is a core problem because it can reduce users to mere observers.

## The Three Dimensions of User Agency and Their Connection to Excessive Guidance

This study defines the concept of “excessive guidance” and analyzes it in connection with three key dimensions of user agency.

- Perceived control: the user’s perception that they are in control during interactions with the environment  
- Voluntary choice: actions and paths voluntarily chosen by the user  
- Decision-making authority: the user’s authority to be involved in final decisions  

When AI guidance infringes on all three, the user degenerates into a being that simply follows the paths presented by the AI.

## Shifting from Attention Optimization to Curiosity-Led Design

The author suggests redefining the role of AI from a “fixed path provider” to an “exploratory collaborator”. Conventional attention optimization focuses on efficiency and accuracy, whereas curiosity-led design centers on natural exploration and learning by the user. This is not merely a change in design philosophy but a structural transformation that enables AI to stimulate and sustain users’ intrinsic motivation.

## Presentation of Measurable Behavioral Indicators

To demonstrate the framework’s practicality, five behavioral measurement indicators are presented. They provide tools for quantitatively evaluating the concept.

- Exploration scope: the breadth of areas accessed by the user  
- Average dwell time: the time spent at each location  
- Path-following ratio: the proportion of recommended paths followed as suggested by the AI  
- Active choice frequency: the number of decisions directly made by the user  
- Recall test performance: the ability to recall content learned in the environment

## Contributions and Limitations of This Study

This study provides a new theoretical tool for HCI research in immersive environments. In particular, it lays a foundation for practical application by defining the negative concept of “excessive guidance” and linking it to measurable indicators. However, with only abstract-level information, specific algorithms, experimental designs, and dataset configurations cannot be verified. Since the paper is centered on a conceptual framework, concrete design guidelines or validated results applicable to real-world implementations require further confirmation through the original text.
