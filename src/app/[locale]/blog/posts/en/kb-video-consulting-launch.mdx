Today I'll write up what I found while developing a non-face-to-face real-name verification service.

## My responsibilities

I am responsible for the real-name verification feature in a channel where customers can get consultations and sign up for products by video, without visiting a branch. Identity verification is legally required for things like remote sales of trust products and remote sign-ups in Star Banking.

## Main considerations

Convenience and security often pulled in opposite directions. Adding verification steps raises the drop-off rate, and removing them raises the risk of identity theft. So I designed the flow to apply different levels of verification at different stages.

## Finding 1: Failure cases are varied

There is one normal path, but the failure cases were varied.

- Glare on the ID card
- Face not detected
- Insufficient lighting
- Communication errors
- Older ID card formats

Giving a different guidance message for each cause of failure took up a major part of the logic.

## Finding 2: How to handle logs

ID images cannot be stored for security reasons, so I designed the logs to record only metadata such as the verification step, similarity score, and retry count instead of the image.

## Finding 3: The need for domain knowledge

Without understanding relevant regulations such as the Real Name Financial Transactions Act, there were many points where I could not judge the right design direction. I learned that understanding domain requirements has to come before the technical implementation.
