## I started by just cutting at 512

Most RAG tutorials start with something like `chunk_size=512, overlap=50`.
I did the same, and the demo worked fine.

The trouble started with real policy documents.

## Problem 1 — clauses get cut in half

Korean financial terms and conditions look like this:

```
Article 15 (Termination)
① The customer may terminate the contract at any time.
② Notwithstanding paragraph ①, termination is restricted in the following cases:
   1. ...
```

Cutting at 512 tokens sometimes splits between ① and ②.
Then retrieval surfaces "may terminate" without "is restricted."
In a financial domain that isn't a quality issue — it's an incident.

## Attempt 1 — bigger overlap

I pushed `overlap` to 200. Fewer cuts, but the index grew and near-duplicate chunks
started crowding the top of the result list.

## Attempt 2 — split by article

```python
import re

def split_by_article(text: str) -> list[str]:
    parts = re.split(r'(Article\s*\d+)', text)
    chunks = []
    for i in range(1, len(parts), 2):
        chunks.append(parts[i] + parts[i + 1])
    return chunks
```

Semantic boundaries held, but length variance became the problem.
Some articles are two lines, some are three pages. Long ones produce mushy embeddings.

## What I settled on — two stages

1. Split by article first (preserve the semantic boundary)
2. If an article is too long, split again by paragraph (①②③)
3. **Prefix every chunk with its parent article heading**

Step 3 was the key.

```
[Article 15 (Termination)] ② Notwithstanding paragraph ①, ...
```

Now even a split chunk keeps its context. Retrieval accuracy improved noticeably.

## What I learned

Chunking isn't a question of "how many tokens." It's a question of
**"what is the unit of meaning in this document?"**

Policies have articles, meeting notes have agenda items, code has functions.
The answer differs per domain. The tutorial's 512 is a default for generic text, not a rule.
