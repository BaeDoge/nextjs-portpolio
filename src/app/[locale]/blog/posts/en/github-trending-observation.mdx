## Why

I was frustrated at hearing about new tools late, so I checked GitHub Trending
weekly for about six months. Daily was too noisy.

## Observation 1 — lots of wrappers

Projects that lightly wrap an existing library show up constantly.
Install them and you often find two or three functions.

Not inherently bad. But I now calculate
"how many lines would this be if I just did it myself?" first.
At fifty lines, doing it myself wins. Every dependency is maintenance debt.

## Observation 2 — stars and maintenance are unrelated

Plenty of 10k-star repos have a last commit eight months old.
Meanwhile some 300-star projects ship weekly.

My order of inspection now:

1. Date of the last commit
2. Closed vs. open issue ratio
3. Number of maintainers (one is a risk)
4. Star count

Stars come last.

## Observation 3 — long READMEs are suspicious

The more badges and comparison tables a project has,
the less likely I could find "why does this exist?"

Good READMEs tend to be short: problem, solution, install, example.

## What I actually adopted

Two things, in six months. A low hit rate,
but those two paid for the whole exercise.

The rest stayed as "so that approach exists too." That's fine as well.
