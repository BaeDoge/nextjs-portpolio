## It started with enthusiasm

Determined to do observability properly, I built a lot of dashboards.
CPU, memory, network, disk IO, GC, connection pools...

A month later, during an actual incident, I opened exactly one of them.

## What I actually use

**1. Error rate**

```
5xx responses / total requests
```

The first thing I check. If this is healthy, things are usually healthy.

**2. p95 latency**

Averages were useless.
A 200ms average hides the users experiencing five seconds.

p95 and p99 immediately expose "some requests are slow."

**3. Throughput**

If request count suddenly hits zero or ten times normal, something is wrong.
I watch **change relative to baseline**, not the absolute number.

## What I deleted

CPU and memory graphs. That may sound odd, but
if either is the real problem it shows up in the three above first.

80% CPU with fast responses isn't a problem.
30% CPU with slow responses isn't a CPU problem.

## I cut alerts too

We once had dozens of alerts a day. Nobody reads those. I didn't either.

I changed the bar to: **"if this fires, must I do something right now?"**

If not, it belongs on a dashboard, not in an alert.
That filter took us to one or two alerts a day — and I started reading them again.

## Now

I'll add more when I actually need it.
The dashboards I built preemptively were never the ones I remembered in a crisis.
