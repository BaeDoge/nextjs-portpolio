Today I'll write up my experience mentoring and serving as a judge.

## What I did

In the KB Youth IT Talent Development Program, I mentored team projects for three weeks and took part as a judge for the final presentations.

## The mentoring process

The feedback I gave most often was a question: "why is this feature needed?" Many teams planned a lot of features and ended up not finishing them, so I worked on narrowing the scope by adjusting priorities.

## What I found while judging

Reviewing more than 20 presentations, I noticed the following tendencies.

**Presentations that scored higher**
- Explained the problem concisely
- The demo worked
- Stated clearly what was unfinished

**Presentations that scored lower**
- Started by listing the tech stack
- Spent too much time on the architecture
- Tried to demo every feature and ran out of time

Technical difficulty did not necessarily match the evaluation, and clear delivery turned out to be an important factor.

## Conclusion

The criteria I saw while judging apply equally to internal presentations at work. I also found that stating clearly what is unfinished actually raises trust.
